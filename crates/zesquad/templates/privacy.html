Privacy

zesquad keeps as little as it can. This is all of it.

If you log in

  • Your Battle.net account id and BattleTag, and when you first and last logged in. Battle.net gives zesquad nothing else: no email, no name, no characters.
  • A session: a random token in a cookie (zesquad_user), kept on the server only as a hash, for 30 days after you last used it.
  • Which squads you started or joined, and when. Members of a squad see each other's BattleTags; nobody else does.
  • While you're away logging in at Battle.net, a cookie (zesquad_login) remembers which page to bring you back to, for ten minutes at most.

You can log out everywhere or delete your account on your account page.

Squads

  • Squads hold what people type in: a name, character names and tags. Character details come from the public Battle.net Armory.
  • A squad can post to a Discord channel. Then zesquad keeps that channel's webhook and sends it the names of characters who join and their new levels, nothing else.
  • A cookie (zesquad_recent) remembers which squads this browser opened, so the front page can list them. It never leaves your browser except to come back here.

Nothing else

No ads, no trackers, no analytics. The server's logs record requests (address, page, time) to keep it running. To slow down anyone hammering it, the server also counts logins and changes per address, in memory only, for an hour at most. zesquad is open source: github.com/tinnet/zesquad.
